Activity panel

The right sidebar's Activity covers one conversation. What a run waits on is never a group: it is the row it concerns, with a red count on its glyph's corner. The one exception is Waiting for you, the reports left for the reader across conversations. Messages are never rows.

Overview

These groups, in this order. Agents is always shown; every other group, header included, is shown only when it has something in it. Only Waiting for you carries a count in its header; Agents adds what its helpers and its scheduled tasks' runs cost. Hovering a group shows its SectionHeader chevron; clicking the header folds it (per-browser, lib/prefs/activity-view.ts).

Page readGET /api/session/{id}/activity/tasks/{task}?filter=all|unfinished|notified&offset=&limit=: the counts, the last 24, the cost and one page. On a conversation with 177 runs it answers in about 26 ms once the conversation is in memory; the first read of a conversation not yet loaded takes about 0.6 s. It is read on entry, on op:execution-update and op:job-status, and every 30 seconds; a refresh replaces the newest rows by id.

Why one row: a task that runs every hour has hundreds of runs, and a row per run, or per line its runs left, buries the agents the reader works with. Peers, as the always-on design compares them, list a task once and keep its history on its own page:

  • Claude Code lists routines.
  • Codex lists its automations.
  • DeepSeek Harness has a tasks page with each task's delivery records.

The row here adds how the last day went, as one strip; the history stays on the task's page, paged and filtered to what did not finish.

Kept apart from Scheduled tasks on purpose: that group holds what the Scheduler does without an agent, so a task is never in both places.

Reading the overview

The overview draws what it last read of the conversation at once and reads again behind it; a new read replaces rows in place and says nothing.

CaseWhat happens
First openAgents is open from the start: the checked-out branch's main agent, at rest with the conversation's last activity. The read adds the other branches, helpers and calls in place.
Coming backEach of the four reads (executions, programs and calls, roster, work) is kept per conversation for the life of the page (lib/execution/activity-reads.ts) and drawn in the first frame, on the tab or after switching conversations. Executions and roster are not read again while fresh: under 30 seconds old, the socket open throughout, and no op:execution-update or op:job-status since the read began. Work is always read, since Scheduler tasks publish no event.
While shownExecutions are read on those events, at most once a second, and every 30 seconds. A failed read is tried again after 2, 4, 8 … seconds, up to 30.
FailureNothing is said while there is something to show. Runs that were never read and keep failing for 5 seconds put "Could not read this conversation's runs." with Retry under Agents.
ServerGET /api/session/{id}/executions reads each session's Jobs once per request (jobs.store.session_jobs_reader) and looks every row's Job up in it, the helpers' usage included; a foreground turn, which has no Job, costs no lookup. The conversation's folder comes from the location index, parsed again only when its file changes. A conversation with 203 runs answers in about 0.2 seconds instead of 4 to 6, and three at once no longer queue on the conversation's lock for 18 seconds.

Pairing an agent call with its sub-agent

The server reads calls from transcripts, not effects, whose metadata carries whole provider payloads. A Job records the reply that spawned it (spawn_caller) and its prompt.

  1. A call with the same caller and prompt started it; calls that did not fail pair first, since a failed call may share its prompt with the retry.
  2. If prompts do not line up, a caller left with one Job and one non-failed call pairs those.
  3. No Job: the call failed to start (result [agent error] … or outcome failed) or did not start.

The caller names the branch: a j_… reply is a sub-agent's, anything else the main agent's.

Pages

A row in the overview opens one page. Pages do not lead to other pages: what belongs to a row opens under it, in place, and stays open until it is closed. Activity at the top, Esc with focus in the panel, or Activity in the rail goes back, restoring the overview's scroll and focus.

Pages move as on a phone, on the Launchpad's spring; with reduced motion they change at once.

The Agent time chart

One vertical time chart, newest on top, with a column for each level: Topics, Messages, Steps, Inside a step. The sidebar's width decides how many columns show, and every column in view is whole.

Sidebar widthColumns
288 (the default, first snap)One, 256 pixels wide
544 (second snap)Two, 256 each
800 (third snap, the maximum)Three, 256 each
Between two snapsThe smaller count, stretched to fill the width

The count is the chart's width over 256, rounded down, at least one and at most three (columnsFor). It is applied in the frame the width changes, so a drag of the handle shows the columns it lands on while it moves.

Every block is a card on hover and can be clicked; a title or line cut short shows in full on hover. A block with more under it leads one column right. A block that is one call, one failure or a message with no steps opens in place, in the run list's words.

An opened block grows, the blocks below move down, and its bars in the columns to the left grow with it. A second click closes it. It grows and closes in one 240 ms move on the standard curve, and its details stay until it has closed over them. A call still running has nothing to open.

The view ends on the deepest column the reader went to, or starts at Topics when that fits. Opening a block whose next column is already in view moves nothing. With fewer columns than levels, the view moves one column at a time:

A move is one slide of the columns' track by its transform, and nothing else:

  • 240 ms for a whole column, less for the rest of one (never under 120 ms), on --ease-standard; a cut with reduced motion.
  • The blocks are laid out for where the move ends before it starts. A column coming in is already at its final size; the clicked block, or the block at the top, holds its place on screen.
  • A column deeper than the one the move ends on is not drawn, even while it slides out.
  • Nothing grows, fades in or settles after the slide.
  • The box never scrolls sideways, only up and down. Blocks more than a screen above or two below the view are not drawn.

Why: the chart used to scroll sideways, with each block's height interpolated from the scroll position. Three things made a move feel wrong:

  • Two motions: the heights finished within the first 64 pixels of a 256-pixel column, while an overdamped spring kept sliding on its long tail and the arriving column faded in on its own curve.
  • Stutter: each frame wrote the top and height of every block, reset the vertical scroll and changed what was drawn, a full layout per frame.
  • Part columns: columns were 256 pixels at any width, and a sideways scroll that was not a swipe settled on a column edge 160 ms after it stopped.

Topics

Each message the user sent has one topic, kept in topics.json beside the session's messages: topics in the order they first appeared, and which topic each message has. Nothing is written to the messages.

  • After each turn a background pass sorts what has no topic, on the same background model as chat titles. It sees the user's words only.
  • A short reply such as "continue" stays with the message before it and costs no call.
  • The first time an older chat's chart is opened, the same pass sorts its messages, 40 to a call. Until then they read Not sorted yet.
  • When a call fails or no model is available, messages stay unsorted and draw with the message before them. The next message tries again; opening the chart tries again after 10 minutes.

openprogram/host/processes/progress.py reads progress from output, most trusted first:

  1. ##progress 45/100 samples lines (the bash tool asks the model to print them for long loops)
  2. the OSC 9;4 terminal progress sequence
  3. tqdm and pip bars
  4. counters such as Epoch 3/10, step 120 of 1000, [45/100]

Once a more trusted source speaks, less trusted ones are ignored, so an inner per-batch bar never overwrites a sample count. Speed and time left are the bar's own, or measured since the count began. A redrawn bar takes one line of the 2,000-line limit, and the agent reading a background command gets each redraw once.

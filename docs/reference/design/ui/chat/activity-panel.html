Activity panel

The right sidebar's Activity covers one conversation. What a run waits on is never a group: it is a red count on the glyph of the agent it concerns. The one exception is Waiting for you, the reports left for the reader across conversations. Messages are never rows.

Overview

Two parts: a few groups, then the chart, where the agents are. Each group, header included, is shown only when it has something in it, in this order. Only Waiting for you carries a count in its header. Hovering a group shows its SectionHeader chevron; clicking the header folds it (per-browser, lib/prefs/activity-view.ts).

The work chart

Under the groups, the conversation's work as one time chart: Topics, Messages, Steps, Inside a step. Rows are topics; reading right goes from a topic to each message in it, and each call. Every agent of the conversation is on it, each drawn once, and listed nowhere else.

While a chart is drawn the groups take at most half the panel's height and scroll among themselves; the chart has the rest and scrolls on its own.

What a message says in each state, and the one button it may carry: Messages on the work chart.

Why topics first. The chart used to start from tasks, and an agent declares a task for most things it does: Tasks had about as many blocks as Messages and said the same twice.

A topic gathers what the reader and the agent did about one thing; the task names each message in it.

Why the agents are only here. The overview used to open with an Agents group, a row per main agent with its sub-agents under it, and the chart had an Agents column before Messages. The same agents were on screen three times. What was tried instead:

Way to say who did itOutcome

Colour, and one glyph per agentKept. The cost: with one agent and no sub-agent, everything right of Topics is one colour.

  • Codex lists its automations.

Reading the overview

The overview draws what it last read of the conversation at once and reads again behind it; a new read replaces rows in place and says nothing.

Pairing an agent call with its sub-agent

The server reads calls from transcripts, not effects, whose metadata carries whole provider payloads. A Job records the reply that spawned it (spawn_caller) and its prompt.

  1. A call with the same caller and prompt started it; calls that did not fail pair first, since a failed call may share its prompt with the retry.
  2. If prompts do not line up, a caller left with one Job and one non-failed call pairs those.
  3. No Job: the call failed to start (result [agent error] … or outcome failed) or did not start.

The caller names the branch: a j_… reply is a sub-agent's, anything else the main agent's.

The Agent time chart

One vertical time chart, newest on top, with a column for each level: Topics, Messages, Steps, Inside a step. The sidebar's width decides how many columns show, and every column in view is whole.

The count is the chart's width over 256, rounded down, at least one and at most three (columnsFor). It is applied in the frame the width changes, so a drag of the handle shows the columns it lands on while it moves.

Every block is a card on hover and can be clicked; a title or line cut short shows in full on hover. A block with more under it leads one column right. A block that is one call, one failure or a message with no steps opens in place, in the run list's words.

An opened block grows, the blocks below move down, and its bars in the columns to the left grow with it. A second click closes it. It grows and closes in one 240 ms move on the standard curve, and its details stay until it has closed over them. A call still running has nothing to open.

The view ends on the deepest column the reader went to, or starts at Topics when that fits. Opening a block whose next column is already in view moves nothing. With fewer columns than levels, the view moves one column at a time:

A move is one slide of the columns' track by its transform, and nothing else:

  • 240 ms for a whole column, less for the rest of one (never under 120 ms), on --ease-standard; a cut with reduced motion.
  • The blocks are laid out for where the move ends before it starts, once. A column coming in is already at its final size; the clicked block, or the block at the top, holds its place on screen.
  • A block on screen whose place or height changed glides there with the slide, in its time and on its curve: the block by its transform, its bar scaled from its top, and words stuck at the top of the view on their own path. These run off the main thread, so nothing is laid out again while they play.
  • A block too far off to be drawn before the move comes in from where the old layout had it, and one that leaves reach rides away before it goes: no block appears or disappears in place while the blocks next to it still move.
  • A column deeper than the one the move ends on is not drawn, even while it slides out.
  • Nothing grows, fades in or settles after the slide.
  • Measured on a conversation of 3 topics and about 40 messages: every frame of a move between columns took about 9 ms, before the glide and with it. Slowed four times, the first move after opening has one frame of about 45 ms either way; the moves after it keep 9 ms.
  • The box never scrolls sideways, only up and down. Blocks more than a screen above or two below the view are not in the layout.
  • The blocks of a column out of view are not in the layout either: only the columns showing, and one on each side while a move is under way, have theirs. A column a move brings in gets its blocks before it shows.
  • The column of the row that has the focus stays in the layout, so the focus and the keys are never dropped; the arrows move among the rows that are in the layout.
  • Measured on a conversation of 8 tasks and about 440 rows, processor slowed four times, moving between columns at three columns wide: the slowest task of a frame went from 252 ms to 45 ms, and a style pass from 2,545 elements (52 ms) to 313 (7 ms).
  • A block's place is written when it changed, and a block is drawn again only when what it says changed, so a chart rebuilt every few seconds while something runs redraws a handful of blocks.

Why: the chart used to scroll sideways, with each block's height interpolated from the scroll position. Three things made a move feel wrong:

  • Two motions: the heights finished within the first 64 pixels of a 256-pixel column, while an overdamped spring kept sliding on its long tail and the arriving column faded in on its own curve.
  • Stutter: each frame wrote the top and height of every block, reset the vertical scroll and changed what was drawn, a full layout per frame.
  • Part columns: columns were 256 pixels at any width, and a sideways scroll that was not a swipe settled on a column edge 160 ms after it stopped.

Topics

Each message the user sent has one topic, kept in topics.json beside the session's messages: topics in the order they first appeared, and which topic each message has. Nothing is written to the messages.

  • After each turn a background pass sorts what has no topic, on the same background model as chat titles. It sees the user's words only.
  • A short reply such as "continue" stays with the message before it and costs no call.
  • The first time an older chat's chart is opened, the same pass sorts its messages, 40 to a call. Until then their topic block is room with nothing drawn.
  • When a call fails or no model is available, messages stay unsorted and draw with the message before them. The next message tries again; opening the chart tries again no sooner than 10 minutes after the last pass, so a pass's own change notice never starts another.

openprogram/host/processes/progress.py reads progress from output, most trusted first:

  1. ##progress 45/100 samples lines (the bash tool asks the model to print them for long loops)
  2. the OSC 9;4 terminal progress sequence
  3. tqdm and pip bars
  4. counters such as Epoch 3/10, step 120 of 1000, [45/100]

Once a more trusted source speaks, less trusted ones are ignored, so an inner per-batch bar never overwrites a sample count. Speed and time left are the bar's own, or measured since the count began. A redrawn bar takes one line of the 2,000-line limit, and the agent reading a background command gets each redraw once.

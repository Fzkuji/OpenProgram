# feedback tasks

The `peer_feedback` task of auto_research (`tasks/feedback/`). Before submission it finds people outside the author list who could read the draft, checks them for conflicts of interest, and drafts a short request to each. Nothing is ever sent: the author reads, edits and sends the drafts, and records the replies in `workflow/feedback.csv`.

Learned from ResearchHub (paid, named human reviewers who work to a deadline) and the pre-submission "friendly review" habit of asking a few colleagues in the field to read a draft.

## peer_feedback

| Item | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| Signature | `peer_feedback(paper_dir: str, count: str = "5", exclude: str = "", out_dir: str = "") -> str` |
| Purpose | The author knows who in the field could give useful feedback before submission, who may not because of a conflict, and has a specific, checked request ready for each. |
| Input | `paper_dir`: the project folder. `count`: how many readers get a draft, 1 to 20 (default 5). `exclude`: comma-separated names or OpenAlex ids (`A…`, or an openalex.org URL) to leave out. `out_dir`: the output folder (default `<paper_dir>/drafts/peer_feedback`). A missing folder, a paper with neither title, abstract nor project.md Claim, or a `count` outside 1 to 20 raises `ValueError`. |
| Process | Gather (code): the title and abstract of the main .tex (project.md's Claim when there is no abstract); the paper's authors from the LaTeX `\author` blocks (and IEEE author blocks), project.md and venue.md `Authors:` and rules.json `required_authors`, as names or OpenAlex ids; three key phrases of the abstract (runs of two or three content words scored by word frequency); the 25 OpenAlex works most relevant to the title and 25 to the key phrases; the paper's .bib entries resolved to OpenAlex works (DOI, arXiv id, close title). Authors are resolved by OpenAlex author search: same name, or same surname and first initial; a namesake who wrote one of the gathered works wins, otherwise the one with the most works, with a note when the choice is unsure. Select (code): every author of the gathered works with weight 1 per work (0.8 for a reference; a team of n > 10 gives each 10/n); the 60 heaviest get their OpenAlex profiles and a score: 0.45 × closeness (weight / 3, at most 1) + 0.15 × recency (newest matched work, 0 after 10 years) + 0.20 × seniority (0.7 × min(h, 30)/30 + 0.3 × min(works, 100)/100) + 0.20 × activity (works in the last three years / 15, at most 1). Conflicts (code, each with its reason shown): an author of the paper; co-authored a work with a paper author in the last five years (the paper authors' recent works, up to 1000); the same current institution as a paper author, one inside the other, or two companies of one group by OpenAlex lineage; named in `exclude`; status `declined` in feedback.csv. Without any resolved author the check uses `exclude` and declines only, and says so. Draft (model): for each of the top `count` without a draft file, one call. Check (code): the cited work must be one of the reader's matched OpenAlex works (schema enum and code) and its exact title must be in the email; an email address in the body is replaced by `[address]`; flagged when the claim takes more than two sentences, the body has flattery words, a number not in the paper, no time frame, or more than 250 words. Contact (code): the OpenAlex page, the ORCID iD and the ORCID record's public researcher URLs (home page before social or publisher profiles); emails are never read, guessed or scraped, and `To:` keeps a placeholder naming the page to look on. |
| Output | JSON with `status`, `report` (candidates.md), `json`, `run`, `tracking`, `candidates`, `excluded`, `drafts`, `authors`, `unavailable_sources`. `FAIL`: no eligible candidate, or no draft could be written or kept. `PASS_WITH_NOTES`: a note (unresolved or ambiguous author, no author at all, a failed draft), an unavailable source, fewer drafts than `count`, or a flagged draft. `PASS` otherwise. Files in `<out_dir>/<timestamp>/`: `candidates.md` (Chinese: authors, ranked table with score parts, excluded people with reasons, tracking), `candidates.json`, `run.json`, `run.md`. In the project: `workflow/feedback/<name>.md` (Chinese header with links, cited work, score and the flags to fix, then `To:`, `Subject:` and the English email) and `workflow/feedback.csv` (`name, openalex, status, date, notes`; status drafted, sent, replied or declined). |
| Effects | Reads OpenAlex (`OPENALEX_API_KEY` when set) and the public ORCID API through `citations.sources.fetch`; a failing source is named and the run goes on. Writes a draft only when its file does not exist, and appends a `drafted` row only for a person not yet in feedback.csv; existing rows, and the author's status edits, are never changed. Sends nothing. The paper is never modified. |
| Model calls | `drafter`: one call per new draft (at most `count`): subject, two-sentence claim, the cited work's id, why this person, the kind of feedback asked, the time frame (two to three weeks, before venue.md's first date when there is one) and the body. |
| Failure | A drafter reply that is malformed or cites a work that is not the reader's leaves that reader without a draft and a note; the rest go on. Rerunning is safe. |
| Tests | `tests/feedback/test_feedback.py`: OpenAlex and ORCID replaced by a fake fetch, the drafter scripted; ranking, every conflict reason (author, co-author, institution, company group, exclude, declined), unresolved names, authors from project.md as ids, the draft flags and the address replacement, kept drafts and author-edited statuses across runs, a cited work that is not the reader's, sources down, and the helpers. |
| Usage | `from auto_research.tasks.feedback import peer_feedback` |

## Not covered

- Registering the task in the program's task list and app.
- Sending the emails or reading the replies: the author does both and edits feedback.csv.
- Conflicts OpenAlex does not record: advisors, grant co-holders, close friends, a reviewer of the paper's earlier version. Put them in `exclude`.
- OpenAlex merges or splits some people's profiles; the ranked table links each profile to check.

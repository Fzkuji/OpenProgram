# paper_review task

The `paper_review` task of auto_research (`tasks/review/`). auto_research calls `paper_review()` on the working tree, or on `draft_dir`, with `reviewer_model`, and keeps the reviews in `drafts/paper_review/`.

Reviews the whole paper as the program committee of its venue would: contribution, rigor and consistency, each weakness with a severity, a section and a fix, and a score. By default three reviewers with different personas review it and an area chair writes the meta-review (after AgentReview); `reviewers=1` gives a single review. It never edits the paper. Rounds happen across runs: the author (or the app's agent) fixes the weaknesses in a draft copy and runs the task on that draft, and the new review says for each earlier weakness whether it is resolved. paper_check checks rules paragraph by paragraph; this task judges the paper as a submission.

| Item | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | A venue-level review of the current paper before submission, with the earlier round's weaknesses tracked. |
| Input | `paper_dir`: the paper's Git repository; `workflow/rules.json` names `main_tex`. `draft_dir`: a revised copy to review instead; empty reviews the working tree. `venue`: the target venue; empty reads the `Venue` and `Requirements` parts of `workflow/venue.md`. `reviewer_model`: the model the reviewer runs on (`provider:model`), so the paper is not reviewed by the model that wrote it; empty uses the caller's model. `out_dir`: the review folder (default `<paper_dir>/drafts/paper_review`). `reviewers`: how many reviewers (1 to 5, default 3). `calibration`: `openreview` (default) grounds the review in real OpenReview reviews (see Calibration); `off` makes no network request. `predictor`: `auto` (default) adds the trained score model's prediction (see Trained score model); `off` skips it. A missing repository, a `reviewers` outside 1 to 5, a `calibration` or `predictor` other than those two, a missing main file, or a main file with no text, raises `ValueError`. |

With `calibration=openreview` and an ICLR/NeurIPS/ICML venue, reads the public OpenReview API v2 (no key). |

## Process

1. **Read.** Code flattens the main file, reads the venue requirements and the newest `review.json` in the review folder. Its weaknesses are the previous round.
2. **Calibrate.** Code fetches the OpenReview anchors (see OpenReview anchors), or none.
3. **Review.** Each `reviewer` sees the paper, the venue and the previous weaknesses; with a panel, also its persona: methodologist (baselines and statistics), outsider to the subfield (clarity and motivation), subfield expert (novelty against prior work), reproducibility, practitioner (significance), in that order. The docstring, the same for all, asks for strengths as well as weaknesses, specific fixes, a faithful score (7 is a clear accept), and a note that a method it names from its own knowledge is a lead to verify. With anchors, the context also holds two or three real reviews (one of an accepted paper, one of a rejected one when there is one) marked as calibration examples not to copy.
4. **Area chair** (panels only). Reads the paper and every review and writes the meta-review. Code checks that every weakness id it cites (R1.W2 ...) is one a reviewer raised.
5. **Report.** Code numbers the weaknesses `W1`, `W2`, ... (with a panel, the consolidated ones, so the next round tracks those), sets the status, adds the acceptance estimate, and writes the review with the anchors section with the previous round's table and, for a panel, each reviewer's review.

## Calibration

Plain code, no model call. The venue family is the first known name in the venue text: ICLR/NeurIPS/ICML (1 to 10, borderline about 6), ACL ARR, including ACL, EMNLP, NAACL, EACL and AACL (1 to 5 overall, about 3.5), CVPR/ICCV/ECCV (1 to 6, about 3.5). The reviewers' mean on the 1 to 10 scale is mapped linearly onto the venue's scale and set against its borderline through a logistic curve that flattens as the scores spread, giving `chance` (in steps of 0.05) and a band: `likely_reject` below 0.3, `likely_accept` above 0.7, `borderline` between. `acceptance_estimate` holds the family, scale, borderline, scores, mean, spread (population standard deviation), mean on the venue's scale, chance, band and a note. It is a rough estimate from scores and typical thresholds, not a prediction of the decision; for any other venue it is `null` and the report says so.

When at least 8 OpenReview anchors have ratings and a decision, both accepted and rejected among them, the table is replaced by an empirical curve: a one-variable logistic regression of accept on each anchor's mean rating (mapped linearly onto the family's scale), fitted by Newton's method in plain Python with a small ridge on the slope so a separable sample stays finite. The chance is that curve at the panel's mean on the venue's scale; `borderline` is then the score where it crosses 50%. `acceptance_estimate` adds `method` (`openreview_logistic` or `venue_table`), `samples` and `accepted` (the decided anchors), and `fit` (`intercept`, `slope`, or `null`). The report names the method used.

## OpenReview anchors

CycleReviewer and OpenReviewer are reviewer models trained on OpenReview reviews; besides the small trained score model below, this task grounds its reviewers and estimate in those reviews (`anchors.py`, plain code).

- **Query.** The paper's `\title` content words, then the abstract's most frequent ones, ten words at most.
- **Where.** Only the ICLR/NeurIPS/ICML family has public reviews. The conference the venue names is searched first, then ICLR, the only one that publishes the reviews of rejected papers. For each, the latest two years (from next year back) that give anchors, up to 5 similar submissions per venue-year (groups such as `ICLR.cc/2025/Conference`).
- **API shape (v2).** Content fields are `{"value": ...}`. `GET https://api2.openreview.net/notes/search?term=<query>&group=<venue id>&source=forum&limit=25` returns submissions; `content.venueid` is the group itself when accepted and `<group>/Rejected_Submission` when rejected; withdrawn and desk-rejected ones are left out. The same search with `source=reply&limit=200` returns replies with their `forum`: official reviews (invitation `.../-/Official_Review`; `rating`, or ICML's `overall_recommendation`, as a number or text such as `6: marginally above ...`; `confidence`, `summary`, `strengths`, `weaknesses`, or NeurIPS 2025's combined `strengths_and_weaknesses`) and decisions (`.../-/Decision`, `decision` such as `Accept (poster)`), joined to the submissions by forum. A decision note wins over `venueid`. `/notes?forum=<id>` answers scripted requests with a bot challenge (403), so it is not used; a submission's mean is over the reviews the reply search returned.
- **Scales.** ICLR to 2025 and NeurIPS 2024 rate 1 to 10, ICLR 2026 0 to 10, NeurIPS from 2025 1 to 6, ICML from 2025 1 to 5; each anchor's mean is also mapped onto the family's scale (`mean_on_family`).
- **Anchor keys.** `title`, `venue`, `group`, `forum`, `link`, `decision` (`accept`/`reject`), `decision_from` (`decision` or `venueid`), `scale`, `ratings`, `mean_rating`, `mean_on_family`, `reviews` (each `rating`, `confidence`, `summary`, `strengths`, `weaknesses`, 500 characters at most).
- **Cache.** `<out_dir>/openreview_cache/<hash of family, conferences and query>.json`; a run with the same query reads it and makes no request. Delete the folder to refetch. Failures are not cached.
- **Failure.** A failed request or a malformed answer gives no anchors and names the source in `unavailable` (such as `OpenReview (ICLR.cc/2026/Conference)`); the review goes on with the table estimate. Other venue families, or a paper without title and abstract, give no anchors with the reason in `skipped`. `calibration=off` makes no request.

## Trained score model

`predictor.py`, plain code with numpy only: a small, local counterpart of CycleReviewer and OpenReviewer that reads only the title and abstract.

- **Data** (`collect(venues, queries, limit)`). ICLR submissions of the latest three decided years (ICLR publishes the reviews of rejected papers), found through the same two search endpoints as the anchors over about 50 broad ML queries (`QUERIES`), one `source=forum` and one `source=reply` request per query and year (`limit=1000`, one second apart), joined by forum. A row has `title`, `abstract`, `ratings` (each mapped onto the family's 1 to 10 scale; ICLR 2026 rates 0 to 10), `rating` (their mean) and `decision` from `venueid` or a decision note; withdrawn, desk-rejected and unreviewed submissions are left out. A mean is over the reviews the reply searches returned, which can be fewer than all. Cached as `~/.cache/auto_research/openreview/dataset-<first>-<last year>.jsonl`; a cached file is read and nothing is fetched.
- **Model** (`train(rows)`). Signed hashing of unigrams and bigrams of title and abstract into 2^18 buckets, sublinear tf, l2-normalised rows, kept sparse in CSR arrays. Rating: ridge regression solved by conjugate gradient on the normal equations (unpenalised intercept). Accept: L2 logistic regression by Newton's method with conjugate-gradient steps. Penalties are picked on a validation slice of the training part; 15% of the rows are held out for the metrics (`rating_r` Pearson, `rating_mae` against `baseline_mae` of predicting the mean, `accept_auc` against 0.5); the saved weights are then refitted on all rows. Saved next to the dataset as `model-<years>.rating.npy`, `model-<years>.accept.npy` (float32, intercept last) and `model-<years>.json` (dims, date, rows, groups, penalties, metrics).
- **Use** (`predict(title, abstract, allow_train)`). Loads the newest saved model; without one, collects and trains when `allow_train` (fewer than 200 rows train nothing). `paper_review` with `predictor=auto` allows training only when `calibration=openreview`; any failure leaves `score_model` `null` and the review goes on. `score_model` holds `rating` (1 to 10), `accept_probability`, `scale`, `metrics`, `trained_on`, `date`, `rows` and `reads`; the report section 训练出的评分模型 says what the model is, how well it did on held-out submissions and that it judges only title and abstract. It does not change the reviewers' scores or the acceptance estimate.
- **Building it by hand.** `python -m auto_research.tasks.review.predictor [budget seconds]` collects (stopping new queries after the budget) and trains.

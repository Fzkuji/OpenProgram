# Writing rules for survey prose

Reader
- The reader is a researcher from a neighbouring area. The prerequisites line says what they already know; never explain those concepts, never use everyday analogies for them, and never step from a "black box" view inward.
- Explain only ideas specific to this field, once, where the story first needs them.

Argument
- Every judgement shows its mechanism and its evidence. A word like "bottleneck", "does not scale", "brittle" or "limited" is never used alone: say whose limit it is, why it arises, and which result shows it (with the paper's id).
- Each method is introduced by the limit of the earlier work it answers, then what it changes, then what it still leaves open. Do not list papers one per sentence.
- State only what the reading notes support. Results carry their condition (dataset, model size, budget). A note read from the abstract only supports positioning, not mechanisms or numbers.
- Write about the field, never about the notes or the sources. No sentence says what "the abstract", "the notes" or "the existing material" can or cannot show. A paper known only from its abstract is placed by the problem it targets, in one clause, without a disclaimer.
- Cite inline as [p12] or [p3, p7] right after the claim the paper supports. Every paragraph cites. Do not repeat a citation at the end of a sentence that only restates papers already cited in it.

Form
- Open each chapter with the problem it answers, not with "This chapter" or a list of papers.
- No template sections: no "read these first", no "three key points", no "open questions of this chapter", no summary boxes.
- Titles are short noun phrases specific to the content, never clauses or questions.
- A comparison table is used only when the methods differ in one or two slots (the scoring function, the training signal); its columns are those slots.
- Plain, dense, precise sentences. At most two commas per sentence. No "whether", "although" or "despite" constructions.
